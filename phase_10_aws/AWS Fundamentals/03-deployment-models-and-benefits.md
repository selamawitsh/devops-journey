# Session 1c · Deployment Models & The 6 Benefits of Cloud

## Deployment Models — Where Does The Cloud Live, Who Can Use It?

| Model | Description | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Public Cloud | Owned by AWS, shared infrastructure, cheapest & most flexible | Most companies (what we use in this course) |
| Private Cloud | Dedicated infrastructure for one org, full control, higher cost | Banks, governments |
| Hybrid Cloud | Sensitive data stays private, everything else goes public | The real-world compromise |

This is a *different* question from IaaS/PaaS/SaaS. Deployment models ask "where and for
whom"; service models ask "how much do I manage."

## The 6 Benefits

1. **Trade CapEx for OpEx** — no huge upfront cost. Example: $500,000 upfront to buy your
   own servers vs. roughly $50 for your first month on AWS.
2. **Economies of scale** — AWS buys hardware at massive scale, so per-unit costs drop, and
   that saving gets passed to you.
3. **Stop guessing capacity** — scale up or down as real demand changes, instead of
   over- or under-provisioning.
4. **Increase speed & agility** — deploy infrastructure in minutes instead of months.
5. **Stop running data centers** — focus your energy on your product, not on racking
   servers.
6. **Go global in minutes** — launch in a new AWS region instead of building a new
   datacenter abroad.

## Why The $500,000 → $50 Story Matters

Buying servers upfront means paying for capacity you might never use, before you even know
if the product will succeed. Cloud flips that: start tiny, pay only for what's actually
used, and if the idea doesn't work out, the financial damage is minimal. This is the same
logic behind why most startups (and most portfolio projects) default to public cloud.

## Key Terms

- **Elasticity** — the ability to scale resources up or down automatically based on demand
- **Region** — an independent, isolated geographic area AWS operates in
